# TN Lens — proposed 30-day public evidence pilot

**Objective:** Produce a weekly research briefing that a public-interest group can use to investigate one Tamil Nadu public-service question, separate substantiated facts from claims, and verify follow-through. **No official affiliation or authority is assumed.**

## Week 1 — Baseline and reproducibility

- Confirm all starter source URLs, publication dates, and attribution.
- Audit the 38-district registry against an official Tamil Nadu current-district list.
- Agree on a small public-data scope: two or three district-level indicators from official NFHS-6/state statistical sources, with page numbers and definitions.
- Import only real measured values; retain collection year, units, geography and caveats.
- Show one source-linked memo to two independent reviewers and record corrections.

**Acceptance:** A reviewer can reach every quantitative source, see its published date and distinguish organiser-reported outputs from independently verified outcomes.

## Week 2 — One reproducible issue investigation

- Use the TASMAC follow-up as a research methods test, not an advocacy conclusion.
- Obtain WTL's public 129-outlet document and official closure/relocation list if available.
- Match each reported outlet only by reliable identifiers and dates; do not publish sensitive location details without review.
- Record supportive, contrary and uncertain sources with written notes.
- Produce a memo that has a separate section for what cannot yet be established.

**Acceptance:** The research remains defensible when an independent reviewer introduces contradictory evidence. A corrected conclusion leaves a visible audit trail.

## Week 3 — Social content analysis without voter tracking

- Request exports or manually verify 25–50 publicly published posts from the organisation's own social channels.
- Label content topic and aggregate post metrics; capture platform-specific denominator caveats.
- Compare content themes with the published monthly programme schedule.
- Do not infer audience residence, political ideology, vote intention, or population representativeness from social metrics.

**Acceptance:** Every imported row opens to a specific public post and analyses can be reproduced from the CSV.

## Week 4 — Global playbook / public-service pilot

- Choose one cooperation-based municipal workflow from FixMyStreet's model (e.g. routing streetlight complaints to an existing official facility).
- Document the local process, data protection plan and consent/agency handoffs.
- Define **before** measures: existing reporting throughput and median acknowledgement time.
- Agree a 30–60-day follow-up plan with a cooperating institution if permission is obtained.

**Acceptance:** A pilot has a clearly named institutional counterpart, baseline metric, measurement method and stop condition. Adoption is not represented as proven impact.

## 2031 horizon — what compounds

Over five years, the useful asset is not a pile of campaign posts: it is a longitudinal, source-linked Tamil Nadu public-issues record with dated claims, institutional responses, tested interventions, corrections and measured outcomes. This should remain transparent, privacy-preserving and useful independently of any election result.

## Decisions needed to proceed beyond v0.1

1. Identify one researcher who will review and correct real source classifications.
2. Provide one authorised social-post export (public-account metrics only).
3. Choose two official district indicators for the first data ingestion.
4. Create a dedicated GitHub repository if shared development or static hosting is desired.

**Not in this pilot:** voter segmentation, microtargeted persuasion, scraping personal profiles, political sentiment inference, turnout modelling, or unsourced strategy forecasts.
