# Decision Note 001 — November 2026 Youth Skilling

**Date:** 23 October 2026  
**Audience:** Prospective WTL leadership research discussion, *not an approved WTL instruction*  
**Purpose:** Clarify the resource-allocation decision before the November youth-skilling activities launch.

## The question

**Should scarce volunteer-hours and partner capacity be dedicated primarily to breadth of youth-skilling events, depth of follow-through, or learning which service format works before replication?**

WTL's published November theme invites programmes across **234 constituencies**, offers **six activity formats**, and specifies **one concrete next step per participant**. This establishes a published programme intention; it does not establish what budget is available, how many events will actually occur or what outcomes follow.

Source: https://www.wetheleader.org/theme-detail?theme=youth-skilling-empowerment

## Why it deserves leadership attention

Participation is easily counted; concrete opportunity creation is harder. Without measuring next-step completion, missing participants, partner capacity and 90-day outcomes, the same report can support several incompatible interpretations of success.

This is also a chance to compare participation through **existing government and institutional skilling pathways**, rather than creating a parallel programme with uncertain employer connectivity.

**Key caveat:** WTL may already measure outcomes privately. Verify the existing workflow before proposing a replacement.

## Three feasible allocation designs — *not ranked*

| Design | Scarce resource primarily used for | What success would mean | Main evidence / trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coverage-led | Local workshops and wider geographic presence | Locations and participants with documented next steps | Broad accessibility; follow-through may be expensive or unknown |
| Partner-led follow-through | Fewer sessions, mentors / employer links, post-event case support | Confirmed completion and tangible opportunities at 30/90 days | Better verification opportunity; limited partner capacity and reach |
| Staged learning | Small comparative pilots, pre-agreed measures and later replication | Evidence about which format performs in which delivery conditions | Stronger information before scale; slower initial expansion |

**No recommendation yet:** there is no verified resource budget, no reliable cost per activity, no partner-capacity register and no comparable outcome baseline.

## The measures to compare

- **Access:** participants served, participation cost, language and travel accessibility (no voter segmentation).
- **Quality:** concrete next steps agreed and completed within 30 days.
- **Outcomes:** verifiable apprenticeship, interview, course completion or paid-work milestones at 90 days; distinguish each kind of outcome.
- **Delivery economics:** spend, volunteer-hours and partner-hours per completed step and per verified opportunity.
- **Evidence gaps:** unknown follow-up outcomes, nonresponse bias, data accuracy and any adverse effects.
- **Institutional feasibility:** which actions WTL can deliver, which depend on government or employer participation, and which can instead use existing channels.

An observed programme milestone is not necessarily a causal effect: do not attribute employment gains to WTL without a suitable evaluation design.

## Four questions for leadership (the real ask)

1. **Outcome:** By 2031, which resident-level results should a youth-skilling programme credibly improve, beyond participation counts?
2. **Constraint:** For November, which is genuinely limiting—money, volunteer-hours, qualified mentors, access to employers or institutional permissions?
3. **Alternatives:** Is there an existing programme already doing part of this job, and what would WTL add that is not otherwise available?
4. **Decision gate:** Which two or three measurable results, with what confidence, would justify continuation, adaptation or discontinuation?

## Offer to the decision-maker

Authorise a **two-week, non-electoral public-development decision analysis**, using consented internal aggregates if available, publicly sourced state data and documented costs. Its deliverables would be:

1. A one-page *options and trade-offs* memo with named uncertainties.
2. A transparent allocation model in which leadership can change budgets and field capacity and inspect alternatives.
3. A 30/90-day measurement protocol for any pilot that is actually approved.

**Not requested now:** access to private member lists, voter databases, social-media DMs, sensitive personal records, or authority to commit money.

## What could reverse the analysis?

- WTL already has accurate and independently reviewed 30/90-day employment measures.
- Existing partners provide reliable job linkage at negligible marginal resource cost.
- A proposed approach imposes meaningful privacy, safety or exclusion risks.
- An alternative public intervention has more credible evidence under the same delivery constraints.
