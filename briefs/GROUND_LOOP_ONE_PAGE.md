# One Next Step. One Ground Signal.

**A practical, chat-enabled proposal for WTL's November 2026 youth-skilling programme**  
Independent TN Lens research concept · 23 October 2026

## The opportunity

WTL already proposes **one practical next step per participant** and invites youth-skilling action across **234 constituencies**. The next layer is a technology-assisted way to make every step useful twice: **help the participant** and **produce a trustworthy signal about what support is missing on the ground**. [WTL programme](https://www.wetheleader.org/theme-detail?theme=youth-skilling-empowerment)

## The five-step loop

**1. Open a chat:** After the event, scan a QR or follow a public link into a Tamil/English mobile web chat. No installation or political membership required. The participant voluntarily opts into the service.

**2. Select one action:** “Prepare a CV,” “Meet a mentor,” “Build a portfolio” or “Explore an apprenticeship.” The chat records a feasible target date and links to an existing provider when appropriate.

**3. Report progress:** On returning, the participant chooses **Started / Completed / Need help**. A specific barrier, such as lack of mentor access, becomes a field support request—not another publicity metric.

**4. Human follow-through:** A permitted field coordinator offers an appropriate referral or support. Completion remains self-reported until reviewed; employment-related milestones are measured separately.

**5. Leadership decision:** TN Lens shows *aggregate* opt-in, next-step, completion and reviewed-outcome counts, unresolved barriers, volunteer-hours and data gaps. Leaders can consider whether the next resources should go to mentors, partnerships or new events.

### Illustrative example — not reported WTL data

One participant chooses “CV review,” then selects “Need a mentor.” The field desk receives a support case. If similar requests recur, the dashboard can show **mentor capacity as an unresolved operational question**. Leadership can test adding qualified mentors, observe completion and costs, and decide whether the response deserves expansion. No individual's phone number or political preferences need to reach the leadership dashboard.

## A concrete first pilot

**One cooperating venue, up to 50 consenting adults** (pilot capacity target, not actual sign-ups), existing WTL event records where available, one trusted coordinating partner, and follow-up at 30 and 90 days. Measure: next-step selection → started → self-reported completed → reviewed completion; separately record interviews, apprenticeships and placements where valid. Show every denominator, missing outcome and volunteer-hour consumed. Do not claim the pilot caused an employment outcome without an appropriate evaluation design.

## Implementation choice and leadership ask

Use a **QR-launched mobile web chat/PWA** connected to a protected case service and an aggregated TN Lens dashboard. Adapt WTL's existing app if it already has compatible capabilities—do not create a duplicate participant register. **Do not assume a WhatsApp Business API bot**: Meta's current policy prohibits the WhatsApp Business Platform for political parties, politicians and campaigns. [Meta policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)

**Decision requested:** permission to examine WTL's existing event workflow and present a two-week technical proof-of-concept, followed by a one-venue, consent-based outcome pilot only after operational and privacy approvals.

**The long-term benefit:** a public-development learning system in which every voluntary next step can inform better service delivery and resource decisions—not voter profiling or campaigning.

*Independent technical proposal. Not authorised by or affiliated with WTL. The current demo is synthetic and browser-local; no WhatsApp, live case service or ground-data feed is connected.*
